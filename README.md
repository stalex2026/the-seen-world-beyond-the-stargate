> The central visual pathway — from the Stargate to the seer.

## Abstract
The standalone companion to Vision: From Photon to Mind, this essay picks up the visual signal at the threshold where the retina ends and the brain begins — the lamina cribrosa, the "Stargate" — and follows the central visual pathway station by station: the optic nerve and chiasm, where two eye-centred images are re-sorted into halves of a world; the lateral geniculate nucleus, a "relay" that spends some ninety per cent of its synapses not on the eye but on descending cortical prediction, brainstem state, and inhibition; V1, the cortex that manufactures the oriented edge and completes the contours that are not there; and the dorsal and ventral streams, where seeing divides into acting and knowing. The anatomy is a scaffold, not the subject. On it the essay builds an ascent — from vision (the optical phenomenon) to seeing (a cortical inference: Helmholtz's "unconscious inference," formalised as predictive coding), to the authored world (perception decoupled from light, in imagery, memory, and dreams), and finally to the seer. It closes at the hard problem of consciousness: having shown how a lit world is assembled from a handful of shadows, it walks up to the one step it cannot take — from the seen world to the felt fact of seeing it — and, by discipline, stops there. Fully referenced (35 Vancouver citations) and illustrated with five figures in the Lapidary house style.
